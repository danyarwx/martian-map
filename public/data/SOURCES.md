| (no file) helmet rocks | **Simulated.** Orbit cannot see rocks under about 1.5 m, so the helmet world scatters them (`src/helmet/world/rocks.ts`) with the rock size-frequency model of Golombek & Rapp (1997, JGR 102, 4117–4129): share of ground covered by rocks of diameter ≥ D is `F(D) = k·exp(−q·D)`, `q = 1.79 + 0.152/k`, diameters 0.15–1.5 m. Rock abundance `k` = 0.05 on smooth floor and 0.12 where the HiRISE ortho is rough (assumed values); no rocks where the sand mask says sand. The same rocks every session (seeded by tile id). **Demo boulder fields are placed for the demo**, not from statistics: 8–14 boulders of 0.5–1.2 m across the planned path 240 m before each stop | 14 Nov 2026 | Helmet view: obstacles for the live computer vision and the re-route |
| (no file) helmet sand look | **Simulated.** The 25 cm ortho cannot show sand up close, so where a tile's sand mask (the row above) says sand the helmet world draws the ground differently (`src/helmet/world/terrainMesh.ts`): about 8 % darker and less red (dark basaltic sand under thin dust), smooth (30 % of the pebble-and-grit shading), and with fine wind ripples 0.22 m apart, all with one assumed crest direction. Picked by eye after rover photographs of ripple fields, not measured | 14 Nov 2026 | Helmet view: what sand looks like to the walker and to the helmet's cameras |
| (no file) helmet sand classifier weights | **Trained on the simulation.** The helmet's sand detector (`src/helmet/cv/sand.ts`) is a logistic regression over 7 numbers per 16 × 16 px image patch (brightness, contrast, gradient energy, gradient direction coherence, redness, height roughness, distance). Its 8 weights (`src/helmet/cv/sandWeights.ts`) were fitted by the eval harness (`src/helmet/eval`, page `#helmet-eval`) on rendered frames of 40 fixed poses, labelled with the sand mask above, and tested on 20 other poses. Training read that ground truth; at run time the detector sees only camera pixels and the same frame's stereo points. It mostly recognises the simulated sand colour above and has not been tested on real photographs | 14 Nov 2026 | Helmet view: sand cells on the hazard map, "Loose sand" warning |

## Numbers that are not files

| Value | Source | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Surface dose rate 0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day | Curiosity RAD, Gale crater: Hassler et al. 2014, Science 343, 1244797. Jezero has no radiation detector | Safety panel (`src/lib/safety.ts`) |
| Earth background 2.4 mSv/year | UNSCEAR 2008 report | Safety panel comparison |
| Career limit 600 mSv | NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 | Safety panel comparison |
| Jezero temperature, pressure, wind | Perseverance MEDA: Rodriguez-Manfredi et al. 2023, Nature Geoscience 16, 19–28 | Safety panel, typical conditions |
| Walking 2.5 km/h up to 20°, rover 10 km/h up to 15°, 8 h outside | Planning assumptions based on Apollo 15–17 surface experience and ISS EVA length (`src/lib/mobility.ts`) | Route time and no-go slopes |
| Walk-back reserve 60 min | Planning assumption (`src/lib/safety.ts`). Apollo's emergency oxygen pack lasted about 30 min at full flow; Apollo traverses were planned inside a "walk-back limit" so the crew could reach the lander on foot after a rover failure. Not a measured Mars value | Walk-back lines on the map, bail-out time per stop, latest turn-back |
| Typical sol: daylight 06:00–18:00, air −80 °C before sunrise to −20 °C at 14:00, gusts and dust devils most frequent 11:00–15:00 | A model, not a forecast (`src/lib/schedule.ts`). Temperature range: Perseverance MEDA, Rodriguez-Manfredi et al. 2023. Midday peak of vortices and gusts: Perseverance MEDA, Newman et al. 2022, Science Advances 8, eabn3783. Daylight: about 12 h at 18° N, shifting by under an hour over the year | Suggested departure time and its reasons |
| Helmet view: sky colour, sun position, shadows, dust haze, far hills | A model, not a measurement (`src/helmet/world/sky.ts`, `farTerrain.ts`). Sun position from plain solar geometry at 10:30 local solar time, latitude of the session start, season assumed to be an equinox (solar declination 0°, near the season Perseverance landed in; the real declination swings ±25.2° over the Mars year). Sky gradient (butterscotch, paler at the horizon) and haze density are picked by eye, not from a measured dust opacity. The far hills are the 10 m grid above (out to 4 km, drawn 2 m low, one flat colour that darkens on slopes); west of about 77.25°E there is no elevation data, so the crater rim there is not drawn | Helmet view: lighting and horizon |
